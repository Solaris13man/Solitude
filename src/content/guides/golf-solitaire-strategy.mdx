Golf Solitaire looks deceptively simple: tap a card, watch it fly to the waste pile, repeat.
But the difference between a casual run and a cleared board is almost entirely about
*planning the order* in which you take cards. This guide explains the rules exactly as
CardHearth plays them, then walks through the principles that actually win games. If you just
want to dive in, you can [play a hand](/golf/) and refer back here.

## The goal, in one sentence

Clear all seven **tableau** columns by moving the bottom-exposed card of any
column onto the **waste pile** whenever it is exactly one rank higher or lower
than the current waste card. When you run out of legal tableau moves, **draw from the
stock** to put a fresh card on the waste and open new possibilities. Empty all seven
columns and you win.

## Does rank wrap around? No.

This is the rule that trips people up, so it's worth stating plainly: **rank does not
wrap around** on CardHearth. A card plays only if it sits exactly one rank above or
below the waste top — a 9 accepts an 8 or a 10, and nothing else. Crucially, an **Ace
does not connect to a King**. If the waste shows an Ace, your only legal play is a 2;
a King will only accept a Queen. Sequences therefore dead-end at both ends of the deck rather
than looping, which is exactly why planning the chain matters so much.

One more rule with strategic weight: **the stock does not recycle**. Once you've
turned a stock card over, it stays on the waste — you never get a second pass. Your draws are
a finite, one-way resource.

## Four principles that win games

### 1. Plan the chain before you commit

A single card on the waste can often start a long run: play a 6 onto a 5, then a 7, then an 8,
and so on. But the **order you take cards changes what's exposed** underneath, so
a greedy first move can cut a run short. Before you tap, trace the sequence: which exposed
cards can follow, and which column they free up next. The best opening move is the one that
leads to the longest unbroken run without needing a draw.

### 2. Prioritise the tallest columns and the cards that open the most

A column you never dig into is dead weight at the end of the game. Favour plays that bite into
the **tallest stacks** early, because they hold the most cards and the most
future options. When two moves look equally legal, take the one that exposes the most useful
next card — the one that keeps your chain alive or unlocks a stuck column.

### 3. Conserve your stock draws

Because the stock is limited and never recycles, every draw is a card you can't get back. Only
draw when **no tableau move helps your plan**. If you can keep a run going by
playing off the columns, do that first; save the stock for the moment you're genuinely stuck
and need a new bridge card to restart.

### 4. Use reversals to extend a run

Because you can move up *or* down by one, you can change direction mid-run: climb from a
5 up to a 7, then come back down through 6 if another 6 is exposed. Spotting these
**reversals** lets you stitch together cards you'd otherwise strand, often
clearing two or three extra cards before you ever touch the stock.

## Common mistakes that lose games

- **Drawing from the stock too early** — the number-one win-killer. Spend
  tableau moves first; a draw you make now is a card you can't make later.
- **Taking a legal card that strands a column**, leaving a card with no
  partner and no way to clear it.
- **Not planning the sequence** — playing the first legal card you see instead
  of the one that opens the longest chain.
- **Forgetting undo exists** — on CardHearth, undo is unlimited, so test a line
  and take it back if it dead-ends.

## Practise the right way

Every CardHearth deal has a number, so you can **replay the exact same hand** to
try a smarter order, or share it with a friend to see who clears it. Because not every Golf
deal is winnable, unlimited *Undo* is your best teacher: explore a chain, see where it
breaks, and rewind to find the line that goes further.
