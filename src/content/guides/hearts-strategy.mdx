Hearts rewards patience and planning far more than luck. If you already know
[the rules](/guides/hearts-rules/), the difference between a good player and a
point-magnet comes down to a handful of habits: passing well, managing the Queen of Spades,
creating voids, and reading when to duck versus when to strike. Here is how to win more.

<div class="tldr">
  <span class="tldr-title">Quick tips</span>
  <ul>
    <li>Pass your danger cards: high hearts and bare high spades.</li>
    <li>Keep low spades as armour against the Queen of Spades.</li>
    <li>Lose tricks on purpose — duck unless you truly must win.</li>
    <li>Make a void early so you have somewhere to dump points later.</li>
    <li>Only chase the moon with a powerhouse hand — and bail the instant it's dead.</li>
  </ul>

## Pass with a plan

The pass is your most important decision, made before you see anything in return. Aim to make
your hand **safe**, not just to dump your worst three cards. Good candidates to pass
are high hearts (the Ace, King, Queen and Jack), and the dangerous spades — the
**Ace and King of Spades**, which can force you to capture the Queen. If you are
going to keep the Queen of Spades, keep enough **low spades** to guard her.

## Respect the Queen of Spades

Thirteen points sit in one card, so half the game is about her. Two truths drive everything:

- **Low spades are armour.** Holding the two through nine of spades means you can
  always follow a spade lead underneath the Queen, so you are never forced to win the trick that
  catches her.
- **High bare spades are a liability.** The Ace or King of Spades with no low
  spades behind them is how you accidentally win the Queen. Pass them or play them off early.

When you don't have the Queen, you want to flush her out: leading spades early can force the
holder to commit, and if you are void in spades you can drop her on whoever wins the trick.

## Create voids early

A **void** — having no cards in a suit — is a weapon. The moment a suit you are void
in is led, you can throw away a heart or the Queen of Spades onto someone else's trick. Use the
pass and the early tricks to shed your shortest side suit so you have somewhere to dump points
later in the hand.

## Duck, don't win

Most of the time you want to **lose** tricks, not win them. Play your highest
*safe* card when you can't be caught, and save your low cards for when a suit gets
dangerous. Counting how many cards of a suit have been played tells you when your King or Queen
of a side suit has become safe to throw — or when it is about to win a pile of hearts you don't
want.

## Control the lead

Leading lets you steer the hand. Early on, lead suits you are **strong and low** in
to draw out high cards safely. Avoid leading a suit where you hold the dangerous top card. Once
hearts are broken, leading low hearts can bleed points onto opponents who are out of the suit.

## Know when to shoot the moon — and when to stop one

Shooting the moon (taking all 26 points) flips the score: you get zero and everyone else gets 26.
Only attempt it with a powerhouse hand — many high hearts, the Queen of Spades with protection,
and high control cards in a side suit. Bail the instant an opponent takes a point you needed.

Just as important is **stopping** someone else's moon. If one player is gobbling up
every point and you can take a single heart to break their run, do it — one point now beats 26
later. Watching for an opponent who is suspiciously winning everything is a defensive skill worth
practising.

## Watch the scoreboard near 100

As scores climb toward 100, play the table, not just your hand. If a trailing opponent is about
to lose, you can afford to take a few points to deny a leader. And if *you* are close to
100, every point matters — tighten up, duck relentlessly, and don't gamble on a half-hearted moon.

## A worked example: the pass

Say you're dealt **&spades;A Q 5, &hearts;A J 7 3, &diams;K 9, &clubs;8 6 4 2**, and you're
passing left. The &spades;A and &spades;Q with only one low spade behind them are exactly how you
accidentally win the Queen of Spades — so pass them, together with the &hearts;A. You keep the
&spades;5 as a duck-under, your remaining low hearts, and the long clubs to follow suit safely. You've
shed your three most dangerous cards before a single trick is played.

## Common mistakes that cost you

- Passing the Queen of Spades but keeping high spades that just win her straight back.
- Holding a bare Ace or King of Spades with no low spades to duck under — the classic way to catch the Queen.
- Winning tricks you didn't have to; taking the lead when ducking was free.
- Committing to a moon shot and bailing too late — or failing to grab a single point to stop someone else's moon.
