Forty Thieves is the patience game for people who want a real fight. Two full decks, strict
same-suit building, and single-card moves combine to make it one of the toughest solitaires in
common play — which is exactly why it has so many devoted fans. This page is a complete
reference to the rules: the deal, the objective, the legal moves, the stock, and how you win. If
you'd rather learn at the table, you can [play Forty Thieves](/forty-thieves/) right
away.

## What is Forty Thieves?

Forty Thieves is a solitaire game played with **two standard decks shuffled together**
— 104 cards in all. It takes its name from the forty cards dealt face up into the tableau at the
start. The table has three areas: ten **tableau columns** where you do your
untangling, a face-down **stock** with its face-up **waste** pile, and
eight **foundations** where the winning piles are built. The double deck and its
strict rules give the game a reputation for being unforgiving, and clearing one is genuinely
satisfying.

## The deal

Shuffle the two decks together and deal **ten tableau columns of four cards each, all face
up**. That is 10&nbsp;&times;&nbsp;4 = **40 cards** on the table, which is
where the name comes from. The remaining **64 cards form the stock**, placed face
down to one side. The eight foundations all start empty. Because every tableau card is face up,
you can read the whole layout from the first move — the only hidden cards are the ones still
waiting in the stock.

## The objective

You win by building all eight **foundations** up by suit, each running from
**Ace to King**. Because you are playing two decks, there are eight foundations —
**two for each suit** — and each fills with thirteen cards. When every foundation
holds a complete Ace-to-King suit, all **104 cards** are home and the game is won.

## Legal moves

Forty Thieves has a short, strict set of rules. The two that define the game are same-suit
building and single-card moves.

- **Tableau builds down in suit.** You may place a card on a tableau column only if
  it is the *same suit* as the top card and exactly one rank lower — a 9&spades; goes
  only on a 10&spades;, never on a 10 of another suit. This is far more restrictive than the
  alternating-colour building you may know from other games.
- **You move one card at a time.** There is no group or sequence move. To shift a
  run you relocate its cards one by one, using other columns and empty spaces as relays and
  rebuilding the run where you want it.
- **Foundations build up by suit from the Ace.** A card goes to a foundation only
  if it is an Ace starting an empty foundation, or the next rank up in the same suit as that
  foundation's top card.
- **An empty column accepts any single card.** When a tableau column is cleared,
  you may place any one card there — a precious resource, since empty columns are your main tool
  for shuffling cards around.

## The stock and waste

When you run out of useful tableau moves, you turn the **stock** one card at a time
onto a face-up **waste** pile. The **top card of the waste is always
available** to play to a foundation or onto a tableau column, but the cards beneath it
are locked until you deal past them. Crucially, there is **no redeal**: once the
stock is used up, it is gone for good. Every flip is a one-way decision, so think before you
dig — a card buried in the waste, or one you fail to use when it surfaces, may never come back.

## Why Forty Thieves is hard

Three rules stack against you. **Same-suit building** means a 9&spades; will only
sit on a 10&spades; — your options at any moment are narrow. **Single-card moves**
mean even a short run takes several careful steps and several free spaces to relocate. And the
**no-redeal stock** punishes any card you misplay or fail to use in time. Taken
together, only a relatively low share of deals are winnable even with good play. That difficulty
is the appeal: every game won is earned, and the planning rewards patience.

## How you win

The game is won the moment all **104 cards** sit on the eight foundations — two
complete Ace-to-King runs per suit, eight in all, every one built cleanly. The tableau and the
waste end up empty and the stock exhausted. Get there and you have beaten one of solitaire's
sterner tests; if you don't, undo is there to back up and try a different line.
