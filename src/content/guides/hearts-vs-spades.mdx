Hearts and Spades are the two most popular trick-taking card games, and they share a lot of DNA:
four players, a standard 52-card deck, 13 tricks per hand, and the rule that you must follow the
led suit. But the goals are opposites. In [Hearts](/hearts/) you're trying to
*avoid* winning the wrong cards; in [Spades](/spades/) you commit to a number of
tricks and have to *deliver*. Here's how they compare.

## What they share

- Four players and a full 52-card deck, aces high.
- Thirteen tricks per hand; you must follow the led suit if you can.
- The game runs over several hands to a target score.

## The core difference: avoid vs deliver

**Hearts is a game of avoidance.** Every heart costs a point and the Queen of Spades
costs thirteen, so you spend the hand ducking tricks and sloughing dangerous cards onto opponents.
The **lowest** score wins. **Spades is a game of contracts.** Before
play, your team bids how many tricks it will take, and you're scored on hitting that number — the
**highest** score wins.

## Partnerships and bidding

**Hearts has no partners and no bidding** — it's every player for themselves, and
the only pre-play decision is the three-card pass. **Spades is a partnership game with
bidding**: you and the player across the table combine your bids into one contract, and
coordinating with your partner (especially covering a [nil bid](/guides/spades-rules/))
is half the game.

## Trump

**Spades always has trump — the spade suit.** A spade beats any other suit, which
creates the whole strategy of ruffing and drawing trumps. **Hearts has no trump at all**;
the highest card of the led suit wins, full stop. If you can't follow suit in Hearts, your off-suit
card can never win — which is exactly how you dump points.

## Scoring and length

Hearts has 26 points on the table each hand (one per heart, 13 for the Queen of Spades) and runs
until someone hits 100 — then the lowest score wins. Spades scores 10 points per bid trick, with
bags and nil bids swinging things, and runs to 500. Spades games tend to run a little longer and
swingier thanks to nil bids and the bag penalty.

## The signature move

Each game has one spectacular play. In Hearts it's **shooting the moon** — take all
26 points yourself and everyone else eats 26 instead. In Spades it's the **nil bid**
— promise zero tricks for a 100-point swing. Both reward nerve and reading the table.

## Which should you play?

- **Play Hearts** if you want a solo, every-player-for-themselves game with tense
  card-counting and no partner to depend on.
- **Play Spades** if you enjoy bidding, planning, and teamwork — predicting your
  tricks and pulling them off with a partner.

Both are free, with no sign-up — so the honest answer is to try both and see which grabs you.
