Euchre is a fast, punchy partnership game played with just 24 cards. Its signature quirks — a
trump suit chosen each hand and two Jacks (the "bowers") that outrank everything — make it feel
different from any other trick-taking game. The team that names trump must take at least three of
five tricks, and the first side to **10** wins. This page is a complete reference. To
learn by doing, you can [play Euchre](/euchre/) now with a computer partner.

## What is Euchre?

Euchre is a four-player game in two **partnerships** — you and the player across the
table against the other two. It uses a stripped-down deck and a trump suit that changes every
hand. Each hand is only five tricks long, so games move quickly and the lead can swing fast.

## The deck

Euchre uses a **24-card deck**: only the **9, 10, Jack, Queen, King and
Ace** of each suit. Everything from the 2 through the 8 is removed. Aces are high (except
where the bowers take over in the trump suit, below).

## The deal

Each player is dealt **five cards**. The next card is turned **face up**
on the table — this "up-card" drives the first round of bidding — and the remaining three cards (the
"kitty") are set aside face down.

## The bowers: trump's special ranks

In the trump suit, two Jacks outrank everything else:

- **Right bower:** the **Jack of the trump suit** — the single highest
  card in the game.
- **Left bower:** the **Jack of the other suit of the same colour**
  (for example, the Jack of diamonds when hearts are trump). It counts as a trump card — not as
  its printed suit — and ranks just below the right bower.

Below the two bowers, the trump suit runs **Ace, King, Queen, 10, 9**. Non-trump
suits rank normally: Ace high down to the 9.

## Choosing trump: ordering up and naming

Bidding happens in up to two rounds, starting to the dealer's left:

1. **Round one — order it up.** Each player may accept the up-card's suit as trump by
   "ordering it up", or pass. If someone orders up, that suit is trump and the
   **dealer picks up the up-card** and discards one card.
2. **Round two — name a suit.** If all four pass, the up-card is turned down and each
   player may now **name a different suit** as trump, or pass. (On CardHearth the
   dealer must name a suit rather than pass it out, so trump is always decided.)

The team that selects trump are the **makers**; they must take at least three tricks.

## Going alone

When you call trump, you may declare that you'll **go alone**. Your partner sets
their cards aside and sits out the hand, leaving you to play one-against-two. The reward is large:
taking all five tricks alone scores **4 points**.

## Playing the tricks

The player to the dealer's left leads the first trick. You **must follow the led suit**
if you can — remembering that the left bower counts as trump, not as its printed suit. If you
can't follow, you may play any card, including a trump. The highest trump wins the trick; if no
trump is played, the highest card of the led suit wins. The winner leads the next trick.

## Scoring

At the end of the hand, score based on the makers' tricks:

- **Makers take 3 or 4 tricks:** 1 point.
- **Makers take all 5 (a "march"):** 2 points.
- **Makers go alone and take all 5:** 4 points.
- **Makers take fewer than 3 (they're "euchred"):** the *other* team scores
  **2 points**.

## How you win

Hands continue, with the deal rotating, until a team reaches **10 points**. Because
being euchred hands two points to the opponents and a lone march grabs four, momentum can flip in
a single hand — which is exactly what makes Euchre so quick and tense.
