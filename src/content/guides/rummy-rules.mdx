"Rummy" is a whole family of matching games built on one simple idea: draw and discard cards until your hand is organised into **melds** — sets and runs — then be the first to get rid of it all. This page covers **basic Rummy** (also called Traditional or Standard Rummy), the version most people learn first, and points you to the popular variants. The most famous variant, [Gin Rummy](/gin/), you can play here free against the computer.

## What is Rummy?

Rummy is played by **two to six players** with a standard 52-card deck (larger groups often use two decks shuffled together). Aces are usually low. The aim is to form your cards into valid combinations and "go out" before anyone else, leaving your opponents stuck with unmatched cards that count against them.

## The deal

Each player is dealt a hand — **10 cards for two players**, 7 each for three or four, 6 each for five or six. The remaining cards form a face-down **stock**, and the top card is turned face up to begin the **discard pile**.

## Melds: sets and runs

A meld is a valid group of three or more cards:

- **Set:** three or four cards of the **same rank** — say three Eights.
- **Run:** three or more cards of the **same suit in sequence** — say the 5-6-7 of clubs.

## Your turn: draw and discard

On your turn you:

1. **Draw** one card — the top of the stock or the top of the discard pile.
2. **Meld or lay off** if you can (optional — see below).
3. **Discard** one card face up to end your turn.

Unlike Gin Rummy, in basic Rummy you put your melds **face up on the table** as you make them rather than keeping them hidden.

## Laying off

Once a meld is on the table — yours or, in many house rules, anyone's — you may **lay off** matching cards onto it: adding the 8 of clubs to a 5-6-7 of clubs run, or a fourth Eight to a set of three. Laying off is how you shed cards quickly toward going out.

## Going out and scoring

You **go out** when you get rid of your last card — having melded or laid off everything, usually finishing with a final discard. The hand ends immediately, and each opponent is penalised for the cards still in their hand: **face cards count 10**, aces typically 1, and number cards their face value. Those points go to the winner (or against the losers, depending on the variant). Games are played to a target score, such as 100 or 250, or for a set number of deals.

## Popular variants

- **[Gin Rummy](/gin/):** the two-player classic — melds stay concealed, and you "knock" once your unmatched cards (deadwood) total 10 or fewer instead of melding your whole hand. See [Gin Rummy vs Rummy](/guides/gin-rummy-vs-rummy/) for the full contrast, or the [Gin Rummy rules](/guides/gin-rummy-rules/).
- **Rummy 500:** you score the value of the cards you meld (rather than penalising leftovers), can draw multiple cards from the discard pile, and play to 500.
- **Canasta, Contract Rummy and others** add wild cards, required contracts, and team play — but all share the same set-and-run core.
