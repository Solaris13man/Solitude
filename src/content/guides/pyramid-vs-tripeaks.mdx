[Pyramid](/pyramid/) and [TriPeaks](/tripeaks/) are the comfort-food solitaires: pretty layouts, short games, easy to pick up. But under the hood they're built on two completely different ideas — one is about *pairing*, the other about *sequencing*.

## How each one works

**Pyramid** deals 28 cards in a pyramid. You remove exposed cards in pairs that sum to **13** (a 5 with an 8, a 4 with a 9; Kings are worth 13 and go alone). The catch is that a card is only available once both cards covering it are gone, so the order you clear things in decides whether the partner you need ever comes free. **TriPeaks** deals three overlapping peaks. You clear one exposed card at a time by taking any card exactly **one rank above or below** the top card of your waste pile — and rank *wraps*, so a King can follow an Ace. String those together and one move flows into the next.

## The feel of each game

Pyramid is the more **deliberate** of the two — a small optimisation puzzle where you're constantly asking "if I take this, do I strand its partner?" TriPeaks is the more **kinetic**: when a streak gets going it's genuinely exciting to watch a single run peel cards off all three peaks. Pyramid rewards foresight; TriPeaks rewards spotting the longest chain.

## Difficulty and winnability

Both are stock-limited, so both include deals that simply can't be cleared — that's the nature of these games, not a flaw. TriPeaks often feels more forgiving because one good streak clears so much at once, while Pyramid can lock up when a needed partner is buried. Skilful order of play wins many marginal hands in each. The [Pyramid strategy](/guides/pyramid-solitaire-strategy/) and [TriPeaks strategy](/guides/tripeaks-solitaire-strategy/) guides show how, and [are solitaire games winnable?](/guides/are-solitaire-games-winnable/) covers where they sit overall.

## Which should you play?

Pick **Pyramid** for a thoughtful little pairing puzzle, and **TriPeaks** when you want quick momentum and satisfying streaks. They're different enough that most people enjoy keeping both around for a spare few minutes — and if you'd rather a game where every deal is guaranteed beatable, try [Sudoku](/sudoku/) or [Mahjong](/mahjong/) instead.
