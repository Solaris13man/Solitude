"Rummy" is a whole family of games built on the same idea: collect cards into **melds**
— sets of the same rank and runs of the same suit. [Gin Rummy](/gin/) is the most
famous two-player member of that family, but it plays quite differently from the basic Rummy many
people learn first. Here's how they compare.

## What they share

- A standard 52-card deck and the same building blocks: sets and runs.
- Draw-and-discard turns that slowly improve your hand.
- The same end goal — organise your cards and get out before your opponent.

## Players

**Gin Rummy is strictly two players.** Basic **Rummy** is flexible —
typically two to six. That difference shapes everything else: Gin is a tight, readable duel where
you can track exactly what your single opponent is doing, while Rummy is a more social,
many-hands-on-the-table game.

## Melding: hidden vs on the table

This is the biggest difference. In basic **Rummy you lay your melds face up** on the
table as you make them, and you can often "lay off" cards onto melds already down. In
**Gin Rummy your melds stay concealed** in your hand until you end the round — your
opponent doesn't see your combinations until you knock or go gin.

## Going out: melding everything vs knocking

In **Rummy** you win a hand by **melding your entire hand**. In
**Gin Rummy** you don't have to: you can **knock** once your unmatched
cards ([deadwood](/guides/gin-rummy-rules/)) total 10 or fewer, or go **gin**
with zero deadwood for a bonus. Gin also has the **undercut** — knock carelessly and a
well-set opponent can turn the tables on you.

## Scoring

**Gin Rummy** scores the difference in deadwood between players, with a 25-point bonus
for gin and another for an undercut, racing to 100. Basic **Rummy** scoring varies by
house and variant (often the winner scores the value of the cards left in opponents' hands), and
popular cousins like **Rummy 500** formalise it further. Gin's scoring is the more
standardised and tactical of the two.

## Which should you play?

- **Want a sharp head-to-head game** with hidden hands, knocking and undercuts? Gin
  Rummy.
- **Want a relaxed game for a group** where melds build openly on the table? Basic
  Rummy.
