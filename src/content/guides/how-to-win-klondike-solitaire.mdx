Klondike is the game most people simply call "Solitaire," and it has a reputation for being largely down to luck. There is some truth to that — not every deal can be won — but the gap between a beginner and a skilled player is enormous. Beginners win perhaps one game in ten; a player who follows a few reliable principles wins close to half their Draw 1 games. This guide explains those principles and, more importantly, *why* they work.

## The goal, in one sentence

Move all 52 cards onto the four **foundations**, building each suit upward from Ace to King. You do that by arranging cards on the seven **tableau** columns into descending runs of alternating colour (a red Nine on a black Ten), drawing fresh cards from the **stock** when you run out of moves. If the rules are new to you, the fastest way to learn is to [play a hand](/klondike/) with hints turned on.

## Five principles that win games

### 1. Flip face-down cards before anything else

Every face-down card you reveal is new information and a new option. When you have a choice between two legal moves, almost always pick the one that exposes a hidden card. Dig into the **tallest piles early** — they hide the most cards, and a column you never open is effectively frozen for the whole game.

### 2. Don't rush cards to the foundation

It feels productive to send every Ace, Two and Three straight up, but those low cards are often the anchors you need to land a run on the tableau. A good rule of thumb: a card is safe to play up once **both** cards of the opposite colour and one rank lower are already on foundations (so you'll never need it to receive a tableau card). Until then, leave it where it can still do work.

### 3. Empty columns are for Kings — and only the right King

An empty column is the most powerful resource in the game: it can hold a King and the long run you build beneath it. But emptying a column to plant the wrong King strands it. Wait for a King that has a Queen of the opposite colour in sight, so the empty space immediately starts a productive run rather than sitting idle.

### 4. Move runs as a unit, and plan the receiving column

Any face-up descending, alternating-colour run can be moved together. Before you shift one, look at where it lands: a move that frees a face-down card or consolidates two columns into one (opening an empty space) is worth far more than one that simply shuffles cards sideways.

### 5. Keep your options open with the waste pile

Don't reflexively play the top waste card just because it's legal. Once you move it, the card beneath becomes available — so sometimes holding back keeps a more useful card accessible. Think one step ahead.

## Draw 1 vs Draw 3

In **Draw 1**, every stock card is reachable on each pass, so you can plan freely and win rates are high. In [**Draw 3**](/klondike-draw-3/), cards turn over three at a time, so only every third card is initially available. The key Draw 3 skill is **counting the stock**: playing a single card from the waste shifts which cards surface on the next cycle, so it can pay to skip a playable card now to reach a better one next pass. Make a habit of going through the whole stock before committing to a line — you'll often spot a card that changes your plan.

## Common mistakes that lose games

- **Banking foundation cards too early** — the number-one win-killer.
- **Ignoring face-down cards** in favour of tidy-looking sideways moves.
- **Emptying a column with no King ready**, wasting your best resource.
- **Burning through the stock without a plan** in Draw 3.
- **Forgetting undo exists** — on CardHearth, undo is unlimited, so explore a line and take it back if it dead-ends.

## Practise the right way

Because every CardHearth deal has a number, you can **replay the exact same hand** to test a different line, or share it with a friend to see who solves it. Use *Hint* when you're genuinely stuck and unlimited *Undo* to learn from a wrong turn — that's how you internalise these principles faster than grinding random deals.
