Gin Rummy is the classic two-player card game of building tidy combinations and getting out
before your opponent. You draw and discard one card at a time, arranging your hand into
**melds** and trimming the leftover **deadwood** until you can safely
end the hand. This page is a complete reference to the rules. To learn by doing, you can
[play Gin Rummy](/gin/) right now against the computer.

## What is Gin Rummy?

Gin Rummy is played by two people with a standard 52-card deck. Aces are **low**.
The aim is to organise your hand into melds and reduce your unmatched cards to a low value, then
end the hand on your terms. Hands are quick, and you play several of them, accumulating points
until someone reaches the target of **100**.

## The deal

Each player is dealt **10 cards**. The next card is turned face up to start the
**discard pile**, and the rest form the face-down **stock**. On each
turn you draw one card and discard one, so your hand is always 10 cards at the end of your turn.

## The first turn

Traditional Gin opens with a small ritual around that first upcard: the non-dealer may take it;
if they decline, the dealer may; and if both pass, the non-dealer draws from the stock instead.
On CardHearth this is simplified — the non-dealer just takes a normal first turn, drawing from
the stock or the discard pile like any other turn.

## Melds: sets and runs

A meld is a valid group of cards. There are two kinds:

- **Set:** three or four cards of the **same rank** — for example,
  three Sevens or four Queens.
- **Run:** three or more cards of the **same suit in sequence** — for
  example, the 4-5-6 of hearts. Aces are low, so the lowest run is Ace-2-3; an ace does not wrap
  around to the King.

A single card can only belong to one meld at a time, so part of the skill is choosing the best arrangement.

## Deadwood

Any card left over that isn't in a meld is **deadwood**. Each deadwood card scores
its pip value: number cards are face value, **face cards are 10**, and
**aces are 1**. Add them up and you have your deadwood total — the number you are
always trying to push down.

## Your turn: draw and discard

On your turn you do two things in order:

1. **Draw** one card — either the top of the face-down stock (unknown) or the top of
   the discard pile (known, but it tells your opponent what you're collecting).
2. **Discard** one card face up onto the discard pile.

Play alternates until someone ends the hand by knocking, or the stock runs too low to continue.

## Knocking

When your deadwood total is **10 or fewer**, you may **knock** to end
the hand (you knock as you make your final discard). You lay down your melds and your deadwood.
Your opponent then lays down their melds and may **lay off** their own deadwood
cards onto *your* melds — extending your run or completing your set — to shrink their own
count. Then you compare deadwood: if yours is lower, you score the **difference**.

## Going gin

If you can meld **all ten cards** with **zero deadwood**, you go
**gin**. This earns a **25-point bonus** on top of your opponent's
deadwood, and crucially the opponent **cannot lay off** any cards. Going gin is
always a clean, safe way to win a hand.

## The undercut

Knocking has a risk. After lay-offs, if your opponent's deadwood is **equal to or less
than yours**, they have **undercut** you. Instead of you scoring, they score
the difference **plus a 25-point undercut bonus**. That is why you don't knock the
instant you're able to — a thin knock can hand the hand to a well-set opponent.

## Scoring and winning

Points accumulate hand by hand: the knocker scores the deadwood difference, a gin adds 25, and an
undercut gives the bonus to the defender. The first player to reach **100 points**
wins the game. If the stock runs too low to continue (only two cards left) before anyone knocks,
the hand is a wash and no one scores. CardHearth plays without the Big Gin variant — going gin
always pays the flat 25-point bonus.
