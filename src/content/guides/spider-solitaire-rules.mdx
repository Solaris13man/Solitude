Spider Solitaire is one of the most popular two-deck solitaire games, and it plays quite
differently from Klondike. There are no separate foundation piles to build onto and suits
matter less than you might expect. This page is a complete reference to the rules — the
setup, the legal moves, the goal, and the suit variants — so you can sit down and play with
confidence. If you would rather learn by doing, you can [play Spider
Solitaire](/spider/) right now with hints turned on.

## What is Spider Solitaire?

Spider is played with **two full decks shuffled together — 104 cards**. The cards
are spread across ten tableau columns, and your job is to sort them into ordered sequences
that can be cleared from the board. Spider comes in three difficulty settings, all using the
same 104 cards: a **1-suit** game (every card is a spade), a
**2-suit** game (spades and hearts), and a **4-suit** game (all four
suits). The more suits in play, the harder it is to build the runs you need.

## Setting up the board

At the start of a game, **54 of the 104 cards are dealt to the ten columns**. The
deal is uneven: the **first four columns receive six cards each** and the
**remaining six columns receive five cards each** (4 × 6 + 6 × 5 = 54). In every
column only the **bottom card — the one nearest you — is turned face-up**; all the
cards above it are face-down.

The **other 50 cards form the stock**, held face-down to one side. Because each
stock deal places one card on every one of the ten columns, those 50 cards give you exactly
**five deals** over the course of the game.

## The objective

The goal is to build **eight complete sequences, each running from King down to Ace in a
single suit**. The moment a column ends in a full, in-suit King-to-Ace run, that run is
**automatically removed from the board** and set aside. Clear all eight runs —
one for each King in the two decks — and you win. There is nothing extra to do once the last
run lifts off: an empty board is a finished game.

## Legal moves

Within the tableau, you build **downward by rank, ignoring suit**. You may place a
card onto any card that is exactly **one rank higher** — a Nine of any suit onto
any Ten, a Two of any suit onto any Three, and so on. This means you can always make progress
even when the colours and suits do not line up.

Suit matters when you want to **move several cards at once**. A group of cards
only travels together if it is a **same-suit run in descending order** — for
example the 8-7-6 of hearts. A sequence of mixed suits is still a legal stack to look at, but
only its single top card can be picked up and moved on its own. Keeping runs in one suit is
therefore what lets you shift large blocks around.

An **empty column accepts any card or run**, regardless of rank, which makes empty
columns a valuable workspace for reorganising tangled stacks. Whenever you move cards off a
column and expose a face-down card beneath, that card is **turned face-up**
automatically.

When you run out of useful moves, you can **deal a new row from the stock**:
tapping the stock lays one card face-up onto each of the ten columns. The one restriction is
that **every column must already hold at least one card** — you cannot deal while
any column is empty, so plan ahead before reaching for the stock.

## Completing and clearing runs

A run is completed when a column ends in thirteen cards of the same suit in order, from
**King at the top down to Ace at the bottom**. As soon as that happens — whether
you formed it by hand or a stock deal completed it — the whole run is lifted off the board and
parked in one of the eight finished slots. Any face-down card it leaves behind is flipped
face-up, ready to use. You have **won the game once all eight runs have been removed**
and the tableau is empty.

## The suit variants and difficulty

The number of suits is Spider's difficulty dial, and CardHearth offers all three on their own
tables:

- **1 suit (easy)** — every card is a spade, so any descending sequence is also
  a same-suit run and moves as a unit. This is the best way to learn the game; start at
  [1-suit Spider](/spider/).
- **2 suits (medium)** — spades and hearts are mixed in, so you have to keep
  suits apart to move blocks. Try [2-suit Spider](/spider-2-suits/) once the basics
  feel natural.
- **4 suits (hard)** — all four suits are in play, making same-suit runs scarce
  and the game a genuine challenge. [4-suit Spider](/spider-4-suits/) is for
  confident players.

For a side-by-side breakdown of how the difficulty changes, see our guide to
[choosing between 1, 2 and 4 suits](/guides/spider-1-2-4-suits/).
