## Hearts — the avoidance classic

[Hearts](/hearts/) is a four-player game where you're trying *not* to win points: every heart costs one and the Queen of Spades costs thirteen, and the lowest score wins. There are no partners and no bidding — just you against three AI players, ducking tricks and occasionally going for the spectacular "shoot the moon". Great for a tense, brainy solo game. Start with the [rules](/guides/hearts-rules/) or the [strategy guide](/guides/hearts-strategy/).

## Spades — bid, partner up, deliver

[Spades](/spades/) pairs you with a computer partner against two AI opponents. Each hand you bid how many tricks you'll take, spades are always trump, and your team races to 500. The bold **nil** bid — promising zero tricks for a big swing — makes it dramatic. Pick Spades when you want bidding and teamwork. See the [rules](/guides/spades-rules/) and [strategy](/guides/spades-strategy/), or compare it head-to-head in [Hearts vs Spades](/guides/hearts-vs-spades/).

## Gin Rummy — quick two-player melding

[Gin Rummy](/gin/) is the sharp head-to-head game: draw and discard to build sets and runs, trim your deadwood, and knock before your opponent does — or go for a clean **gin**. Hands are fast and the lead changes constantly. Ideal when you want a tight duel rather than a four-player table. Learn the [rules](/guides/gin-rummy-rules/) and [strategy](/guides/gin-rummy-strategy/).

## Euchre — fast partnership trumps

[Euchre](/euchre/) is a punchy partnership game on a short 24-card deck, famous for its two bower Jacks that rule the trump suit. Hands are only five tricks and games run to 10, so it's quick and swingy — and you can "go alone" for a big score. Read the [rules](/guides/euchre-rules/) and [strategy](/guides/euchre-strategy/), or weigh it against Spades in [Euchre vs Spades](/guides/euchre-vs-spades/).

## Which one should you play?

- **Solo and brainy?** Hearts.
- **Bidding and teamwork?** Spades.
- **Quick one-on-one?** Gin Rummy.
- **Fast and punchy?** Euchre.
