[Hearts](/hearts/) and [Euchre](/euchre/) are both trick-taking games, but
that's nearly where the resemblance ends. Hearts is a solo game of *avoiding* points with no
trump suit; Euchre is a fast partnership game built around a chosen trump and two all-powerful
Jacks. Here's how they line up.

## What they share

- Four players and follow-suit trick-taking.
- Played over several hands to a target score.
- A standard pack of cards (though Euchre uses only part of it).

## The deck and pace

**Hearts uses the full 52-card deck** and deals 13 cards each, so a hand is 13 tricks.
**Euchre uses a 24-card deck** (9 through Ace) and deals 5, so a hand is just five
tricks and a game to 10 finishes fast. Euchre is the quicker, punchier game; Hearts hands take
longer and build more tension.

## Trump and the bowers

**Hearts has no trump** — the highest card of the led suit wins, and an off-suit card
can never take a trick (which is how you dump points). **Euchre is all about trump:**
a suit is chosen each hand, and the **right bower** (Jack of trump) and
**left bower** (the same-colour Jack) become the two strongest cards in the game.
Learning the bowers is central to Euchre and has no parallel in Hearts.

## Solo vs partnership, and the goal

**Hearts is every player for themselves**, and the aim is to take as *few*
points as possible — the **lowest** score wins. **Euchre is a partnership**
game where your team calls trump and tries to *win* at least three of the five tricks. One
game punishes winning the wrong cards; the other rewards winning the right ones.

## Scoring and the big play

Hearts puts 26 points on the table each hand (hearts and the Queen of Spades) and runs to 100, with
**shooting the moon** as its dramatic swing. Euchre scores 1–4 points a hand and runs
to 10, with **going alone** — benching your partner for a shot at all five tricks — as
its bold move.

## Which should you play?

- **Play Hearts** for a solo, no-trump game of careful avoidance and card-counting.
- **Play Euchre** for a quick partnership game with trump, the bowers, and fast swings.

Both are free with no sign-up, so try each and see which fits your mood.
