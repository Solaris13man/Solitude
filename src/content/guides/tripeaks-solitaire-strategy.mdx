TriPeaks is a fast, friendly game with surprising depth. On the surface you're just tapping
cards that are one rank away from the waste pile — but the difference between clearing a board
and stalling out comes down to one idea: **the streak**. Beginners take whatever
move is in front of them; skilled players plan the longest possible chain before they ever
touch the stock. This guide explains how to read the board and, more importantly, *why*
each habit works.

## The goal, in one sentence

Clear all three **peaks** by removing exposed cards that are exactly one rank
higher **or** lower than the current waste card. Rank wraps around, so an Ace
links both a King and a 2. A card becomes available the moment nothing covers it, and removing
a card can uncover one or two more beneath it. If the game is new to you, the fastest way to
learn is to [play a hand](/tripeaks/) and watch how each removal opens the board.

## Five principles that win games

### 1. Streaks are the heart of the game

Every consecutive card you remove without drawing from the stock builds a longer chain — and a
longer chain scores far more than the same cards cleared one at a time. So before you tap
anything, ask: **what is the longest run I can make right now?** A chain like
5-6-7-8 or 9-8-7-6 cleared in one streak is worth more than four separate pickups, and it
saves your stock for later. Plan the run first, then execute it.

### 2. Look ahead before you commit

The order you remove cards changes what gets uncovered, so trace the chain in your head before
you start it. A 7 might be playable two different ways, but only one of them keeps the run
going afterward. **Follow each possible chain to its end** and pick the path that
stays alive longest or that frees the most new cards. This look-ahead is the single skill that
separates a high score from a stall.

### 3. Clear the peaks evenly and open two cards at once

Don't tunnel into one peak while the others stay tall. Spread your removals so all three peaks
come down together — a lopsided board leaves you with fewer playable cards later. When you have
a choice, **prefer the card that uncovers two new cards** rather than one: every
extra exposed card is another link your next chain might use.

### 4. Draw from the stock only when you must

The stock is a limited resource, and every draw **resets your streak to zero**.
Treat a draw as a last resort: exhaust every chain on the board first, and only flip a new
waste card when you genuinely have no legal move. Drawing early is the most common way players
throw away both points and cards they'll wish they had.

### 5. Use the Ace's wrap to bridge chains

Because rank wraps, the **K-A-2** link lets you connect the top and bottom of the
rank order. If a chain stalls at a King, an Ace can carry it onward to a 2 — and vice versa.
Keep an eye on exposed Aces and Kings: they're often the hinge that turns two short, dead runs
into one long, scoring streak.

## Common mistakes that lose games

- **Drawing from the stock too early** — the number-one streak-killer.
- **Removing a card that uncovers nothing useful**, when another move would have opened the board.
- **Not looking ahead** for the longer chain, so you break a run that could have continued.
- **Tunnelling into one peak** and leaving the others too tall to finish.
- **Forgetting undo exists** — on CardHearth, undo is unlimited, so test a line and take it back if it dead-ends.

## Practise the right way

Because every CardHearth deal has a number, you can **replay the exact same board**
to test a different chain, or share it with a friend to see who finds the longest streak. Use
unlimited *Undo* to explore a line and learn from a wrong turn — that's how you train
your eye to read chains faster than grinding random deals. Bear in mind that **not every
deal is winnable**, so a loss isn't always a mistake.
