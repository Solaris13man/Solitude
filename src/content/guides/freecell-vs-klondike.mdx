[FreeCell](/freecell/) and [Klondike](/klondike/) are the two most-played
solitaire games in the world, and on the surface they look almost identical: build four
foundations up by suit while arranging cards into descending, alternating-colour runs. But the
two play in fundamentally different ways. Pick the wrong one and you'll either feel cheated by
luck or frustrated by a puzzle that never lets you off the hook. This guide lays out the real
difference so you can choose the game that fits how you want to play.

## The core difference: hidden cards vs full information

**Klondike** deals most of its tableau face-down. Only the bottom card of each
column starts face-up, and you draw the rest of the deck from a **stock** a card
(or three) at a time. That means you are always working with hidden information — you don't know
what's underneath until you dig, and you can't always reach a stock card when you need it. The
result is a genuine luck element: not every deal can be won. Around 80% of Klondike deals are
winnable in theory with perfect play, and noticeably fewer in practice.

**FreeCell** deals every single card **face-up** from the start, and
gives you four **free cells** — single-card holding spots you use as temporary
storage while you untangle the board. There is no stock and nothing hidden. That turns FreeCell
into a pure planning puzzle: the answer is always somewhere on the table, and almost every deal
is solvable. Of the classic 32,000 numbered deals, only deal **#11982** has been
proven impossible.

## How they compare, point by point

- **Luck vs skill:** Klondike mixes skill with a real luck of the draw; FreeCell is
  almost entirely skill, because nothing is hidden.
- **Winnability:** FreeCell deals are nearly always solvable (just one of the
  classic 32,000 is not); Klondike tops out around 80% even with flawless play.
- **How a loss feels:** In Klondike you can always wonder whether the deal was
  simply unwinnable. In FreeCell a loss is almost always your own misplay — humbling, but it's
  how you improve.
- **Session length:** Klondike hands are quick and easy to abandon; FreeCell games
  tend to run longer because you're solving rather than reacting.
- **Learning curve:** Klondike is faster to pick up and play on autopilot; FreeCell
  asks more upfront but rewards you with steady, visible improvement.

## How it feels to lose

This is the difference most people underestimate. When you lose at Klondike, you can legitimately
shrug it off — perhaps the deal was one of the roughly one-in-five that simply cannot be won, and
no amount of skill would have helped. That makes Klondike forgiving and low-pressure. FreeCell
offers no such comfort. Because the board is fully visible and almost every deal is solvable, a
loss means you missed the line. For some players that's exactly the appeal; for others it's the
reason to reach for Klondike on a tired evening.

## Which should you play?

**Pick FreeCell** if you want a fair, always-solvable test of planning where the
outcome is entirely in your hands and losses are on you, not the cards. It's the better choice if
you enjoy thinking several moves ahead and want a game that rewards getting better at it.

**Pick Klondike** if you want the familiar, casual classic — the game most people
mean when they say "Solitaire." It's quick, comfortably played on autopilot, and the touch of
luck keeps every deal feeling fresh without demanding deep concentration.

And you don't have to choose just one. Plenty of people keep both in rotation: Klondike to unwind,
FreeCell when they want a real puzzle. Once you know how each game wants to be played, the deeper
tactics are in our [FreeCell strategy guide](/guides/freecell-strategy/) and our
