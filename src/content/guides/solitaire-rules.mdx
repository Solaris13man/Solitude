Klondike is the game most people simply call "Solitaire." It is a one-player **patience** game played with a single deck, and the version that shipped with early home computers made it the most-played card game in the world. This page is a complete reference to the rules: how the cards are dealt, what you are trying to do, exactly which moves are legal, how the stock and scoring work, and how a game ends. If you would rather learn by doing, you can [play a hand](/klondike/) right away.

## The setup

Klondike uses one **standard 52-card deck** (no Jokers). After shuffling, the cards are dealt into four areas of the table:

- **The tableau** — seven columns dealt left to right. The first column gets one card, the second two, the third three, and so on up to seven in the last column — 28 cards in all. In every column, only the **top (last-dealt) card is face-up**; all the cards beneath it are face-down.
- **The foundations** — four empty spaces, one for each suit. They start the game with no cards and are built up over the course of play.
- **The stock** — the 24 cards left over after the deal, placed face-down as a draw pile.
- **The waste** — an empty pile next to the stock that receives the cards you turn over from it.

## The objective

You win by building all four **foundations**. Each foundation holds one suit and is built **up in sequence, starting with the Ace**: Ace, then Two, Three, and so on through Jack, Queen, and finally King. When all four foundations are complete — 13 cards each, 52 in total — the game is won.

## Legal moves

On your turn you may make any move that the rules allow, in any order, for as long as legal moves exist. There are five kinds:

### Building on the tableau

You build the tableau columns **downward in rank and in alternating colours**. A card may be placed on a tableau card that is one rank higher and the opposite colour — for example, a red Nine goes on a black Ten, and a black Queen goes on a red King. (Red suits are Hearts and Diamonds; black suits are Clubs and Spades.)

### Moving runs as a unit

A sequence of face-up cards that is already in correct order (descending, alternating colour) is called a **run**, and you may move the whole run at once. The card at the bottom of the run still has to follow the alternating-colour rule for wherever it lands. Face-down cards can never be moved.

### Filling an empty column

When a tableau column is emptied, only a **King** — alone, or as the top of a run led by a King — may be moved into the empty space. No other card can start a new column.

### Playing to the foundations

The top card of any tableau column, or the top card of the waste, can be moved to a foundation whenever it is the next card that foundation needs (the matching suit and the next rank up). Only **one card at a time** may go to a foundation. You may also move a card back *off* a foundation onto the tableau if it helps, though this is rarely useful and costs points (see scoring).

### Flipping an exposed card

Whenever a move uncovers a face-down card at the bottom of a tableau column, that card is **turned face-up** and becomes available to play. On CardHearth this happens automatically.

## Drawing and recycling the stock

When you run out of useful tableau moves, you turn cards from the **stock** into the **waste**. In **Draw 1** you turn one card at a time; in [**Draw 3**](/klondike-draw-3/) you turn three at a time, and only the top card of those three is immediately playable. The top of the waste is always available to play onto the tableau or a foundation.

When the stock is empty, you can **recycle**: the waste is turned face-down and becomes the stock again, so you can pass through the cards once more. Draw 1 allows unlimited recycles, and so does Draw 3 — though, as the scoring below shows, recycling is not free.

## Scoring

CardHearth uses the standard Klondike scoring system. Points are awarded and deducted as follows:

- **+10** for every card moved to a foundation.
- **+5** for moving a card from the waste to a tableau column.
- **+5** for flipping a face-down tableau card face-up.
- **−15** for moving a card back from a foundation to the tableau.
- **Recycling the waste:** in Draw 1, every recycle costs **−100** points. In Draw 3, the first two recycles are free; from the **third** recycle onward, each one costs **−20**. Your score never drops below zero from a recycle.

Moving a card from one tableau column to another scores nothing in itself — but if it uncovers a face-down card, you collect the +5 for the flip.

## How you win — and when a game ends

You **win** the moment all 52 cards sit on the four foundations, each suit complete from Ace to King. Often the game reaches a point where every card is face-up and the stock and waste are empty; from there the outcome is decided, and CardHearth can **auto-finish** the deal for you.

A game **ends without a win** when no legal move remains — no card can go to a foundation, no tableau move helps, and cycling the stock turns up nothing playable. Not every Klondike deal is solvable, so a dead end is part of the game. On CardHearth you can use unlimited *Undo* to back out of a wrong turn, or start a fresh deal.

## Common variations

**Draw 1 vs Draw 3** — both playable on CardHearth — changes how many stock cards you turn at once: Draw 1 is the gentler, higher-winning game, while Draw 3 demands you plan your stock passes. The other variation you'll meet elsewhere is **Vegas scoring**, a money-style system in which you "buy" the deck for 52 points and earn 5 points per card sent to a foundation, aiming to finish ahead. CardHearth sticks with the standard cumulative scoring described above — but the rules of play are identical either way.
