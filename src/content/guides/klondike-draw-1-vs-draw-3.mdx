When you start a game of Klondike, one setting changes everything: how many cards turn over
from the stock at a time. **Draw 1** turns a single card; **Draw 3**
turns three. It sounds like a small difference, but it splits Klondike into two genuinely
different games — one relaxed and winnable, one tense and demanding. This guide explains how
they differ, what changes in scoring, and which one you should pick.

## How the two modes differ mechanically

In **Draw 1**, every time you tap the stock, exactly one card flips onto the
waste pile. That means **every card in the stock is reachable on each pass**.
Nothing is hidden behind another card, so you can see your whole deck over the course of a
single cycle and plan around it. This is why Draw 1 is so much more forgiving: if a card you
need is buried, you simply keep drawing until it surfaces, and a strong player wins roughly
**35–45% or more** of deals.

In [**Draw 3**](/klondike-draw-3/), three cards flip at once and only
the top of that group is playable. That means **only every third card is initially
available** — the two cards beneath each turned card are out of reach until you cycle
back around. The essential Draw 3 skill is **counting the stock**: because
playing a single card from the waste shifts which cards land on top during the next cycle, a
smart player will sometimes skip a playable card now to bring a more useful one to the surface
on the next pass. This planning requirement is what gives Draw 3 its classic, tougher feel and
its much lower win rate of around **10–15%**.

## The key differences at a glance

- **Stock access** — Draw 1 reaches every card each pass; Draw 3 reaches only
  every third card until you recycle.
- **Win rate** — Draw 1 is roughly 35–45%+ for a strong player; Draw 3 is
  around 10–15%.
- **Skill demand** — Draw 1 rewards solid fundamentals; Draw 3 adds stock
  counting and pass planning on top.
- **Feel** — Draw 1 is relaxed and steady; Draw 3 is the traditional,
  Vegas-style challenge.

## What changes in scoring

The points you earn for moves are identical in both modes: 10 points for a card sent to a
foundation, 5 points for waste-to-tableau moves and for flipping a face-down tableau card, and
a 15-point penalty for pulling a card back off a foundation. The one scoring difference is the
**cost of recycling the waste**. In Draw 1, recycling the waste pile costs
**100 points** every time. In Draw 3, the first three passes are free and each
pass **after the third costs 20 points**. The design nudges Draw 3 players to be
efficient with their stock cycles rather than spinning through the deck endlessly.

## Which should you play?

Pick **Draw 1** if you want to relax, you are learning the game, or you simply
like winning more often. With every card reachable each pass, you can focus on good habits —
flipping face-down cards, holding back low cards, saving empty columns for the right King —
without the extra burden of stock counting. It is the friendliest way to enjoy Klondike and
the best place to build your fundamentals. If you are new, start here and
[play a hand](/klondike/) with hints turned on.

Pick **Draw 3** when you want a real challenge and the traditional, Vegas-style
game. The lower win rate is the point: every solved deal feels earned, and the extra planning
layer keeps the game interesting long after Draw 1 starts to feel routine. If you already win
comfortably in Draw 1, Draw 3 is the natural next step.

You do not have to commit to either one. You can **switch modes anytime in Settings**,
and the change applies to your next deal. Both modes share everything else that makes
CardHearth pleasant to play: the same **numbered, shareable deals** — so you can
replay the exact same hand or send it to a friend — and the same **unlimited undo**,
so you can explore a line and take it back if it dead-ends. That makes it easy to try the same
deal in both modes and feel the difference for yourself.
