Scorpion is a digging game. You build long suited runs right in the tableau, but the catch is that the cards you need are usually buried — and Scorpion's signature move lets you haul whole stacks around to free them. It rewards patience and a bit of nerve. This page is a complete reference to how Scorpion is played: the deal, the goal, the moves, and how you win. If you'd rather learn by doing, you can [play Scorpion](/scorpion/) right away with undo and hints to back you up.

## What is Scorpion Solitaire?

Scorpion is a solitaire game played with a single standard 52-card deck. It is a close cousin of **Spider**: you build sequences **down in suit** and aim to assemble full King-to-Ace runs. What sets Scorpion apart is the move rule — you may pick up *any* face-up card along with everything on top of it, in whatever order, and shift the whole group at once. There are no separate foundations to play to; the winning runs are built in place, right in the tableau.

## The deal

Scorpion lays out **seven tableau columns of seven cards each** — 49 cards in all. The deal is partly hidden: in **columns one to four**, the bottom three cards of each column are dealt **face down**; **columns five to seven** are dealt **entirely face up**. The **three remaining cards** form a small **face-down stock** set to one side. So you start with a mix of visible and hidden cards, and part of the puzzle is exposing what you can't yet see.

## The objective

You win by arranging all the cards into **four suited sequences**, each running from **King down to Ace** — one complete run per suit. These runs are built **within the tableau**; there is no separate pile to move them to. When all four same-suit King-to-Ace runs are assembled, the game is won.

## Building down in suit

Scorpion builds **down in suit**. You may place a card on another card only if it is the **same suit** and **exactly one rank higher**. A 9&spades; goes onto a 10&spades;, a Queen&hearts; onto a King&hearts;, and so on. Colour and the other suits don't help here — it is strictly suit-matched. Over the game these matched cards link up into the long descending runs you need to win.

## Moving cards — the Scorpion twist

Here is the rule that gives Scorpion its sting. You may pick up **any face-up card together with every card stacked on top of it** — even if that group is a jumbled, out-of-order mess — and move the whole pile onto a same-suit card one rank higher. The cards on top don't have to be in sequence; only the bottom card of the group you lift has to fit its new home.

For example, suppose a 7&clubs; you need is buried under a 2&hearts; and a J&spades;. If there is an 8&clubs; available elsewhere, you can lift the 7&clubs; *with* the 2&hearts; and J&spades; riding on top and drop the entire stack onto the 8&clubs;. The 7&clubs; is now placed correctly, and you can deal with the two passengers later. This is how you dig out cards that would be stuck for good in stricter games — you simply relocate whatever is sitting on them.

## The three-card stock

The three leftover cards are your **stock**, and you get to use them **once**. When you deal the stock, the three cards go out face up, one onto each of the **first three columns**. There is **no recycling** — this deal happens a single time and never again. Because it is one-shot, time it well: deal the stock when those three fresh cards are likely to open up moves rather than bury more cards you need.

## Empty columns

Clearing a column gives you room to manoeuvre, but an **empty column can only be filled by a King** (and any cards riding on top of it). Nothing else may start a fresh column. That makes empty columns valuable and a little awkward: hold one open for a King with a useful run already attached, rather than emptying a column when you have no King ready to move in.

One handy side effect of all this shuffling: whenever you move the cards sitting above a **face-down card**, that card **flips face up**. Exposing hidden cards is most of the work in Scorpion, so look for moves that turn cards over even when they don't immediately extend a run.

## How you win

The game is won the moment the tableau holds **four complete suited runs**, each from King down to Ace. Since the runs are built in place, there is no final flurry of moves to a foundation — once the last four King-to-Ace sequences are assembled, you're done. Scorpion is a tougher, more tactical relative of Spider, so expect to lean on undo while you learn which columns to dig into first.
