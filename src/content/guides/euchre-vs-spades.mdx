[Euchre](/euchre/) and [Spades](/spades/) are both four-player partnership
games built on trump and trick-taking, so they're natural to compare. But one is a short, snappy
game driven by two special Jacks, and the other is a longer game of careful bidding. Here's how
they line up.

## What they share

- Four players in two fixed partnerships (you and the player opposite).
- A trump suit and follow-suit trick-taking.
- A bold solo option — going alone in Euchre, bidding nil in Spades.

## The deck and hand length

**Euchre uses a 24-card deck** — only the 9 through Ace of each suit — and deals
**five cards** each, so a hand is just five tricks. **Spades uses the full
52-card deck** and deals **thirteen**, so a hand is thirteen tricks. That
alone makes Euchre much quicker per hand.

## Trump: a chosen suit and the bowers

In **Spades, trump is always spades** — simple and fixed. In **Euchre, trump
is chosen each hand** by ordering up or naming a suit, and the two Jacks become the
powerhouse cards: the **right bower** (Jack of trump) is the highest card in the
game, and the **left bower** (the same-colour Jack) becomes the second-highest trump.
Learning to value the bowers is the heart of Euchre and has no equivalent in Spades.

## Bidding

**Spades has precise bidding:** every player names exactly how many tricks they
expect, and your team is scored against that combined contract. **Euchre's "bidding" is
simpler:** one team just commits to making trump and promises to take at least three of
five tricks. Spades asks for an exact number; Euchre asks "can your team win the hand?"

## Scoring and game length

**Euchre** scores 1 point for taking 3–4 tricks, 2 for all five, and 4 for a lone
sweep — and the first team to **10** wins, so games are short. **Spades**
scores 10 per bid trick with bags and nil swings, running all the way to **500**.
Euchre is a quick best-of; Spades is a longer campaign.

## Which should you play?

- **Play Euchre** if you want fast, punchy games, the unique bower mechanic, and
  quick momentum swings.
- **Play Spades** if you enjoy precise bidding, longer games, and the deep
  partnership play of covering a nil.

Both are free with a computer partner, so you can learn either in a few minutes.
