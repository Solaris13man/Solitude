Euchre is decided at the call. If you already know
[the rules](/guides/euchre-rules/), winning comes from judging your hand correctly
before you name trump, valuing the bowers, and playing tight defence when the other team makes
it. Here's how.

## Count your hand before you call

Calling trump makes your team the **makers**, and you must take at least three of
five tricks or get euchred for two points. Before you order up or name a suit, count the tricks
your hand can win *in that trump*:

- **The right bower** (Jack of trump) is an almost-certain trick.
- **The left bower and the Ace of trump** are strong winners.
- **Off-suit aces** often take a trick before they can be trumped.
- **Number of trumps:** three trumps, or two trumps plus an off-suit ace, is usually a call.

If you can credibly see three tricks, call. With one lone low trump and nothing on the side, pass
and let the other team risk it.

## Value the bowers correctly

The bowers warp the whole hand. The **right bower beats everything**, and the
**left bower** — the same-colour Jack — plays as a trump, not as its printed suit.
That means your "off-suit" Jack of the matching colour is really a second top trump, and the suit
it appears to belong to is one card shorter than it looks. Count it as trump when you bid and when
you follow suit.

## Think about who holds the up-card

In the first bidding round, ordering up gives the **dealer** the up-card. If the
dealer is your **partner**, ordering up a strong card effectively hands them a trump
— often worth doing. If the dealer is an **opponent**, you're improving their hand,
so you need to be more confident in your own before you order it up.

## Know when to go alone

Going alone benches your partner and lets a clean sweep score **4 points** instead of
2. Only do it with a commanding hand — typically the right bower plus the left bower or the Ace,
and ideally an off-suit ace to lock up a non-trump trick. If even one trick looks shaky, take your
partner along and settle for the standard score.

## Lead trump when you're strong

As the maker with good trump, **leading trump** draws it out of the opponents' hands
so your later winners are safe. With the right bower and length, pull their trumps early. If your
trump is thinner, lead off-suit aces instead and save your trumps to ruff.

## Play defence to euchre them

When the other team calls, your goal is to take three tricks and score two points. Make the
makers **spend their trumps**: lead off-suit aces to force ruffs, and hold a bower or
high trump back to steal a trick at the end. Count the trumps that have appeared — once the bowers
and aces are gone, a modest trump of yours may suddenly be the boss card.

## A worked example: should you go alone?

Clubs are trump and you hold **&clubs;J (right bower), &spades;J (left bower), &clubs;A, &clubs;K,
and &hearts;A**. That's four trumps — including both bowers — plus an off-suit ace. The two bowers
and the &clubs;A pull every trump the opponents have, the &clubs;K mops up, and the &hearts;A takes a fifth:
a near-certain sweep, so **go alone** for 4 points. Swap the &clubs;K for a junk &diams;9 and a
clean sweep is no longer likely — take your partner along and settle for the probable 2.

## Common mistakes that cost you

- Calling trump on one bare, low trump and getting euchred — that hands the other team 2 points.
- Forgetting the left bower counts as trump, so you misjudge your trump length (and follow suit wrong).
- Wasting the right bower early when leading it would have drawn out the opponents' trumps.
- Going alone on a hand that can only see four tricks — same 1 point, but you benched your partner.
