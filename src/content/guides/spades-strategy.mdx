Spades is won at the bid. If you already know
[the rules](/guides/spades-rules/), the leap to winning comes from counting your hand
honestly, playing as a partnership, and respecting the bag penalty. Here is how to do all three.

<div class="tldr">
  <span class="tldr-title">Quick tips</span>
  <ul>
    <li>Bid the tricks you can actually win — when unsure, bid the lower number.</li>
    <li>Getting set costs 10 per bid trick; a bag only costs you later. Underbid, don't overbid.</li>
    <li>Stop taking tricks once your team has made its bid — ten bags is -100.</li>
    <li>If your partner bid nil, cover them: win the tricks they can't safely duck.</li>
    <li>Save high spades to capture aces and control the end of the hand.</li>
  </ul>

## Count your bid honestly

Your bid should reflect tricks you can **actually win**, not tricks you'd like to.
Start by counting your near-certain winners:

- **High spades:** the Ace and King are almost always tricks; the Queen often is.
- **Side-suit aces:** usually good for a trick on the first round of that suit.
- **Protected kings:** a King with at least one small card behind it will often score.
- **Trumping power:** a void or singleton in a side suit, plus a few low spades to
  ruff with, is worth roughly an extra trick.

When unsure between two numbers, **bid the lower one**. A bag costs you a future
penalty at worst; getting *set* costs you ten points per bid trick right now.

## Play as a partnership

Your two bids combine into one contract, so think about the team's total, not your own tricks.
If your partner bid high, you may only need to chip in one or two — don't fight them for tricks
they're set up to win. If your partner bid **nil**, your job changes entirely: you
must **cover** them. Win tricks they can't safely duck, lead suits that let them
throw their dangerous cards, and take the high cards so a forced win never lands on them.

## Respect the bags

Overtricks feel like free points, but ten bags wipe out 100. Once your team has made its bid,
stop grabbing tricks you don't need — play **under** when a trick is already going to
your side, and dump high cards you'd otherwise be stuck winning with later. Keep an eye on the
running bag count; near 10, ducking becomes a priority over a single extra point.

## Manage your spades

Spades are your trump and your currency. Don't burn high spades early on side-suit tricks you
could win another way — save them to capture opponents' aces and to control the end of the hand.
Leading spades to **draw** them out can be powerful when you hold length, stripping
opponents of trumps so your side-suit winners run. But remember you can't lead spades until
they're broken.

## Set the opponents

Defense wins games too. If the other team bid aggressively, you don't always need your own tricks
— you need *theirs*. Force them to use trumps early, hold up your high cards to deny a key
trick, and count how many they still owe. Setting a team that bid 8 swings 80 points your way in
relative terms. Watch for an opponent's nil, too, and try to feed them a trick they can't avoid.

## Adjust to the score

Late in a game to 500, bid for the situation. If you're well ahead, bid conservatively and play
safe — don't hand the trailing team a chance to set you. If you're behind, look for a bold nil or
an aggressive bid that can close the gap in one hand. And always know how close each team is to
the next bag penalty.

## A worked example: counting your bid

You pick up **&spades;A K 4, &hearts;A, &diams;K 7 3, &clubs;Q 9 6 2**. Count your real
winners: the &spades;A and &spades;K are two near-certain tricks, the singleton &hearts;A is a third
(and leaves you void to trump hearts later), and the &diams;K behind two small diamonds will often be a
fourth. That's a solid **bid of 4** — not 5. The &clubs;Q is not a reliable trick, so don't
count it; if it scores, it's a bag, which is a far cheaper mistake than getting set.

## Common mistakes that cost you

- Overbidding to look strong — being set costs 10 points per bid trick, far more than a stray bag.
- Grabbing overtricks you don't need; every 10 bags wipes out 100 points.
- Fighting your partner for tricks they already have covered.
- Leaving a partner's nil exposed — failing to win the trick they can't duck.
