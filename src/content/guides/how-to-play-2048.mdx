2048 is one of those games that takes about thirty seconds to learn and a lot longer to master. If you've watched someone swiping tiles around a grid and wondered what's going on, this guide is for you. We'll cover exactly what 2048 is, the handful of rules that govern every move, how you win, and how a game ends — no strategy jargon, just the basics. When you're ready, you can [play 2048](/2048/) right here in your browser.

## What is 2048?

2048 is a **sliding tile puzzle** played on a 4×4 grid — sixteen cells in total. Each cell is either empty or holds a numbered tile, and every number is a power of two: 2, 4, 8, 16, and on up. You start with two low tiles already on the board, and your job is to keep combining them into bigger numbers. It's a single-player game with no timer and no opponent — just you, the grid, and a steadily filling board.

## The rules

There are only three things to know, and they apply on every single turn:

- **Every move slides the whole board.** When you swipe or press an arrow key, *all* the tiles slide as far as they can in that one direction at once. You don't move a single tile — you shift the entire grid left, right, up, or down.
- **Matching tiles merge.** When two tiles with the same number slide into each other, they **merge into one tile equal to their sum**: 2 + 2 → 4, 4 + 4 → 8, 8 + 8 → 16, and so on. A tile can only merge once per move, so a row of four 2s becomes two 4s, not a single 8.
- **A new tile appears after every move.** As long as a move actually changes the board, a new tile — usually a **2**, occasionally a **4** — spawns in a random empty cell. If a swipe wouldn't move or merge anything, it doesn't count, and no new tile appears.

## The goal

The objective is right there in the name: combine tiles until you create a single tile showing **2048**. Getting there means stacking merges on top of merges — two 1024s make the 2048, two 512s make a 1024, and so on down the chain. Reaching 2048 is the official win, but it isn't the end of the road: you can **keep playing for higher tiles** like 4096 and 8192 if you want to push your score as far as it will go.

## How you lose

There's only one way to lose. The board fills up over time because a fresh tile arrives after almost every move, and the game ends when **the board is full and no merges remain** — meaning no two neighbouring tiles share a number. At that point, no swipe in any direction can change anything, so the game is over. The trick to staying alive is keeping matching tiles next to each other so you always have a merge available to free up space.

## The controls

Moving is as simple as it gets. On a computer you use the **arrow keys** (or WASD); on a phone or tablet you **swipe** in the direction you want the tiles to go. CardHearth supports both keyboard and touch, so the game feels natural whether you're at a desk or on the couch. Two extras make CardHearth's version friendlier for learning:

- **Undo.** Made a swipe you regret? Take it back. Undo lets you reverse a move and try a different direction — perfect while you're still getting a feel for how the board reacts.
- **Numbered, shareable boards.** Every game has a number that fixes the exact sequence of tiles, so you can replay the same board or share it with a friend and compare how you each handled the identical run.

## Getting started

Once the rules click, one habit makes an immediate difference: **keep your biggest tile parked in a corner** and build toward it, rather than letting your high numbers drift around the middle. That's the heart of the "corner strategy," and it's the foundation almost every strong 2048 player relies on.
