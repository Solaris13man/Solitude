TriPeaks Solitaire is a fast, single-deck game built around chains: you clear
cards one at a time by stepping up or down in rank, trying to knock down three
pyramids before the stock runs out. The rules are simple enough to learn in a
minute, but a few details — how the cards overlap, when rank "wraps," and how
scoring rewards long runs — are worth getting exactly right. This page is a
complete reference. When you're ready, [play a hand](/tripeaks/) to
see it in motion.

## What is TriPeaks Solitaire?

TriPeaks (also written Tri-Peaks or Three Peaks) is a single-deck
**open-style** solitaire. Most of the tableau is dealt face up, so
there's little hidden information — the challenge is sequencing your moves to
keep a chain going rather than uncovering secrets. Each turn you remove one
exposed tableau card whose rank is adjacent to the card on top of the waste pile,
and the goal is to clear the whole tableau.

## The setup

The tableau is dealt as **three peaks of 28 cards**, stacked in four
rows so that each row overlaps the one above it by half a card:

- **Row 1 (the peaks):** three single cards, one at the top of each peak.
- **Row 2:** six cards — two tucked beneath each peak.
- **Row 3:** nine cards spanning the bases of the peaks.
- **Row 4 (the base):** ten cards, dealt **face up**.

That's 28 cards in the formation. One more card is turned face up to start the
**waste pile** (the active card you build from), and the remaining
**23 cards form the stock** you draw from when you get stuck. Only the
base row begins face up; the cards above it are revealed as the two cards covering
them are cleared. A tableau card is **available** (playable) only when
nothing overlaps it from the row below — that is, when both cards directly covering
it have been removed.

## The objective

Your goal is to **clear all three peaks** — every one of the 28 tableau
cards. There are no foundations to build and no suits to sort; success is simply an
empty formation. If the stock runs dry and no legal move remains while cards are
still on the peaks, the deal is lost.

## Legal moves

On your turn you may remove any **available** tableau card whose rank is
exactly **one higher or one lower** than the card currently on top of
the waste. Suit and colour never matter — only rank. The card you play moves onto the
waste and becomes the new card to build from, which may expose fresh cards behind it.

Rank **wraps around**: Ace connects to both King and 2. So onto a 2 you
can play an Ace or a 3; onto an Ace you can play a King or a 2; onto a King you can
play a Queen or an Ace. This wrap is what makes long chains possible — a run can climb
or fall straight through the King-Ace boundary, such as Queen → King → Ace → 2.

When no available card is adjacent to the waste, **draw from the stock**.
The drawn card flips face up onto the waste and becomes the new build card. Drawing is
always legal as long as stock remains, but it comes at a cost: each draw
**resets your streak** (see below). There is no recycling in TriPeaks — once
the 23-card stock is exhausted, that's all the help you get.

## Scoring and streaks

TriPeaks rewards momentum. Every card you clear without drawing builds a
**streak**, and the streak sets the value of each clear. The first card in
a run is worth **10 points**, the second **20**, the third
**30**, and so on — the score added always equals the current streak length
times ten. A four-card chain therefore scores 10 + 20 + 30 + 40 = 100 points, far more
than the same four cards cleared with draws in between.

The catch is the reset: the moment you **draw from the stock**, the streak
drops back to zero and the next clear starts again at 10. That single rule is the heart
of TriPeaks strategy — every draw you can avoid keeps a bigger multiplier alive.

## How you win

You win when **all three peaks are cleared** — every tableau card removed to
the waste. Because you only ever draw 23 stock cards and there's no recycle, you can't
simply grind through the deck; you have to keep finding playable cards before the stock
empties. Like most solitaires, **not every deal is winnable**: some shuffles
leave a card stranded with no legal path to it. Skilful play — chaining efficiently and
prioritising the cards that unblock the peaks — is what turns a winnable deal into a win.
